SIDES (the car's own left and right)
Left and right always mean the insured car's own sides, as its driver sitting in it and facing forward sees them, never the left or right of the photo. Cars here are right-hand drive: the driver sits on the car's right.
Step 1. Which end of the car faces the camera?
- Front: headlamps with clear lenses, grille and badge, bonnet with the windscreen behind it, front number plate.
- Rear: tail lamps with red lenses, boot lid or tailgate, rear windscreen, exhaust pipe, reversing camera.
- Singapore plates: a WHITE plate is on the front, a YELLOW plate on the rear; a black plate can be on either end.
- No end faces the camera (a straight side view): look at where the car's front points.
Step 2. Read the side from this table.
- Front faces the camera: the photo's LEFT half is the car's RIGHT; the photo's RIGHT half is the car's LEFT.
- Rear faces the camera: the photo's LEFT half is the car's LEFT; the photo's RIGHT half is the car's RIGHT.
- Side view, the car's front points to the photo's LEFT: you see the car's LEFT side.
- Side view, the car's front points to the photo's RIGHT: you see the car's RIGHT side.
- Corner (three-quarter) view: use the row of the end that faces the camera for everything in the photo, the side panels too. Example: headlamps toward you and a dented door on the photo's right: the door is on the car's LEFT.
Step 3. Check with a second clue when there is one.
- The steering wheel, seen through the side glass, is on the car's RIGHT. From inside the cabin facing forward, it is on the photo's right.
- A close-up of one lamp, reflector or bumper end: find the car's centre beside it (grille, badge, number plate, boot-lid edge). If the centre is to the part's right in the photo, the part is in the photo's left half; then use the table.
- The fuel flap: only when a wide shot of this case already showed which side it is on.
- A close-up with no clue shows the same side as the wide shots just before it (lower photo numbers).
- Do not use mirror shape, wiper position or shadows: they differ between car models.
If two clues disagree, or there is no clue, use the part's '(side undetermined)' name: a wrong side is worse than none.
